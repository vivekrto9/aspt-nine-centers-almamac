# Design Philosophy — Field Almanac

## Visual thesis

Field Almanac treats Human Design as a carefully indexed reference publication: navy ink, ruled off-white paper, disciplined grids, serif editorial hierarchy, and small monospaced annotations. The experience should feel researched, calm, tactile, and trustworthy.

## Principles

1. Use rules, columns, numbering, and whitespace to establish hierarchy before decoration.
2. Use Zilla Slab for editorial statements, Work Sans for reading, and DM Mono for labels and data.
3. Keep the palette narrow: `#16203C` ink, `#E7E9EF` ground, `#F6F7FA` paper, `#2B45C8` definition, and `#B62F49` annotation.
4. Prefer square corners, thin rules, dotted dividers, and print-like surfaces over shadows or rounded cards.
5. Reflow the publication grid on mobile while preserving readable measure and the order of evidence.
6. Keep the Content Studio’s peacock/copper chrome visually independent from the customer theme.

## Avoid

- Cobalt staggered layouts, risograph offsets, warm editorial ornament, gradients, or generic SaaS cards.
- More colors, fonts, radii, or shadows than the reference requires.
- Decorative motion that makes the page feel less like a stable reference document.
- Changing BodyGraph geometry or visitor-flow behavior for visual reasons.

## Interaction thesis

Motion is quiet and functional: brief state reveals, clear accordion/type transitions, and restrained hover color changes. Reduced-motion preferences remove non-essential transitions.
